How an episode works

Every city runs through the same seven steps, in the same order, so you can compare one place against another.

  1. The house

    The typical home price, and what 20% or 10% down really means in cash.

  2. Monthly cost

    Mortgage at this week's rate, plus local property tax and insurance.

  3. Salary needed

    The lender rule worked backwards: the income it takes to qualify.

  4. Take-home

    What that salary keeps after federal, state and payroll taxes.

  5. Who can buy

    Real median pay for common local jobs, alone and as a couple.

  6. Rent vs own

    The typical rent next to the cost of owning, month by month.

  7. Cheaper nearby

    Where the same paycheck buys more, with the trade-offs.

Then we show what moves the number most: a one-point drop in rates, a smaller first home, two incomes, or putting less down.

Where the numbers come from

Every statistic comes from a public source, and every source is listed in that video's description. Mortgage rates refresh weekly; the government datasets refresh each year when new releases come out.

Read the full methodology

  • Census Bureau American Community Survey (home values, rents, incomes, property taxes)
  • Bureau of Labor Statistics wages by job and metro (OEWS)
  • Freddie Mac mortgage rate survey
  • BEA Regional Price Parities (cost of living)
  • FHFA House Price Index
  • IRS and state tax rules
  • Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED)

Questions

Is this real estate or financial advice?

No. It's general education: one clear way to run the numbers for a typical home in a typical situation. Your price, rate, credit, debts and taxes will differ, so check current numbers with a lender before you buy.

Why use the 28% rule?

Many lenders look for your housing payment to stay under about 28% of your gross income, and your total debts under about 36%. It's a common yardstick, not a law, and some loans allow more. We show the math so you can plug in your own number.

Why are home values lower than listing prices I see?

The Census figure is the typical value owners report for all homes, not just the ones for sale right now, and it lags today's market a little. Homes listed today in hot areas often cost more, so treat our numbers as a floor.

Can I request a city?

Yes. Comment the city you want and the job you'd use to buy there on any episode. We cover the largest US metros first and update every city each year.
