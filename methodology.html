How we calculate

The assumptions behind every episode, in one place.

Each episode works through the cost of buying the typical home in one US metro area, using public data and the same rules every time. These are the rules.

The home and the payment

ItemAssumption
Home priceMedian home value for the metro area (Census ACS). Owners report it, so it can lag current sale prices.
Down payment20% in the main case. We also show 10% down with mortgage insurance.
Mortgage rate30-year fixed rate from Freddie Mac's weekly survey (via FRED), refreshed every week.
Property taxThe metro's typical property tax paid as a share of home value (Census ACS).
Homeowners insuranceA stated estimate: 0.5% of the home's value a year, adjusted in a few states (higher in Florida, Louisiana, Texas and Oklahoma). Real quotes vary a lot.
Mortgage insurance (PMI)0.5% of the loan a year when less than 20% is put down.

The salary you need

Many lenders want your housing payment (principal, interest, taxes and insurance) to be no more than about 28% of your gross income. We work that backwards: monthly cost × 12 ÷ 28%. This assumes no other debts. Car loans or student loans push the number higher, because lenders also cap total debts at around 36% of income.

Take-home pay

Take-home uses current federal income tax brackets and the standard deduction for a single filer, plus Social Security, Medicare and the state's income tax. It does not include retirement contributions, health insurance premiums or local taxes, so real paychecks are usually a bit smaller.

Who can afford it

Pay for each job is the median annual wage for that occupation in that metro area, from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (OEWS). Half the people in that job earn more and half earn less. Couples are two medians added together.

Rent and cost of living

Rent is the metro's median gross rent (Census ACS). Day-to-day costs use the Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parities, where the US average is 100.

Every episode is educational. It shows one reasonable way to run the numbers for a typical home, not a recommendation for anyone's own purchase.

Sources

Every statistic quoted in an episode is listed, with its source and date, in that video's description. Main sources: U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey, Bureau of Labor Statistics OEWS, Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey, Bureau of Economic Analysis Regional Price Parities, FHFA House Price Index, and the IRS and state revenue departments.
